Arcas is free, and these are the few rules that come with it. Last updated 5 November 2026.

Arcas is free and comes as it is. Check what it changes before you rely on it, keep an invite code to yourself, and read the privacy page for what leaves your PC.

Free and open source

Arcas is a free Excel add-in made by John Nguyen. Its code is open source under the MIT License, so you may use, copy and change it, at home or at work, for any number of people, at no cost.

No warranty

Arcas is provided as is, with no warranty of any kind, as the MIT License says. John is not liable for any loss or damage that comes from using it.

Check before you rely on it

Arcas can flag correct values and miss mistakes. A suggestion changes nothing until you accept it. You are responsible for checking each change before you accept it, and for checking important figures yourself before you rely on a sheet it has looked at.

AI suggestions

Ask AI and Check words with AI are optional. Their suggestions come from Google's Gemini and can be wrong, even when they sound sure. Read each one before you accept it.

Invite codes

An invite code is for you alone: don't share it, post it or sell it. Each invite has a daily cap, and John's relay is for help with Excel fixes only. Don't try to get around the caps, send other work through the relay or overload it. John can turn off any invite at any time, and there is no promise the relay is up.

Your data

The privacy page says what stays on your PC, what AI help sends and to whom, and what this website counts.

Changes and contact

John may update these terms. When he does, the date at the top changes, and every earlier version is in the project's history on GitHub.

Questions about these terms go through the support page.
